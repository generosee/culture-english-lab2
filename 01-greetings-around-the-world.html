3. Reading

Every day, people around the world greet each other, but they do not all do it the same way. A greeting is a small action, but it says a lot about a culture.

In Japan, people often bow. A small bow is fine with friends, but a deeper bow shows more respect, for example to a teacher or a boss. In the United States and many European countries, a handshake is common when you meet someone for the first time. People usually look the other person in the eye, because this is seen as honest and polite.

In France and parts of South America, friends sometimes kiss each other lightly on the cheek. In Thailand, people press their hands together and make a small bow. This gesture is called the “wai.” In Taiwan, many people simply nod, smile, or wave, and they often ask, “Have you eaten?” as a friendly hello.

No custom is better than another. When you travel or meet people from other countries, watch what they do and follow them. A smile works almost everywhere!

4. Check Your Understanding

  1. Why do some people in Japan make a deeper bow?
    Show answer

    To show more respect, for example to a teacher or a boss.

  2. What is common in the United States when you meet someone for the first time?
    Show answer

    A handshake, usually while looking the person in the eye.

  3. What is the “wai,” and where do people use it?
    Show answer

    Pressing your hands together with a small bow. People use it in Thailand.

  4. What friendly question do people in Taiwan sometimes ask as a greeting?
    Show answer

    “Have you eaten?”

5. Speak & Act

Greeting Role-play (pairs): Your teacher gives each pair a country card. Practise greeting each other the way people do in that country, then add two English lines:

A: “Hi, nice to meet you. I'm …”
B: “Nice to meet you too! In my country, we usually …”

Perform for the class. The class guesses your country!

“If I visit ______, I will greet people by ______ because ______.”
